Abstract

This report documents the design and development of a full-stack ecommerce
website for Orient Computer, a Bangladeshi retailer selling PC components, UPS
and IPS systems, batteries, solar products, audio-visual equipment, and office
supplies. The company had no online sales channel before this project.

The application was built with React (Vite), Node.js, Express.js, and MongoDB.
Customers can browse the product catalogue, filter by category and brand, search
by name or SKU, add items to a cart, and complete checkout. Accepted payment
methods are Cash on Delivery, manual bKash, and manual Nagad, all of which
are common for Bangladeshi online stores. Registered customers can view their
order history, track shipments, manage a wishlist, and write reviews on delivered
products.

Administrators work through a separate dashboard to manage products, categories,
brands, inventory, orders, and delivery charges. The payment verification workflow
allows staff to confirm or reject bKash and Nagad transactions based on the
transaction ID and screenshot the customer submits. All business rules, including
stock checks, price freezing at purchase time, and coupon validation, run server-side
so they cannot be bypassed from the browser.

The backend API is deployed-ready for Vercel or Railway, and the database runs
on MongoDB Atlas. The frontend builds to a static bundle deployable to Netlify
or Vercel.


Keywords: MERN stack, ecommerce, Bangladesh, bKash, Nagad, React, Node.js,
MongoDB, JWT authentication, role-based access control

Chapter 1        Introduction


1.1     Overview and background
Orient Computer is a computer and electronics retailer in Dhaka, Bangladesh. The
company sells PC components, UPS and IPS systems, batteries, renewable-energy
equipment, audio-visual products, telecom hardware, and office supplies. Before
this project the company had no ecommerce channel. Customers either visited one
of its showrooms or contacted sales by phone.

Moving sales online is not straightforward for a retailer in Bangladesh. International
payment gateways are not universally available, customers expect to pay by mobile
money (bKash and Nagad) or on delivery, and the delivery charge depends on
whether the order is going inside Dhaka or to another district. The application had
to handle all of this correctly from the start rather than treating it as an optional
extra.

My internship task was to design and build a working ecommerce website for Orient
Computer from scratch, covering the customer-facing storefront, the checkout flow,
and the admin management panel.


1.2     Objectives
The main objectives of the project were:

   1. Build a product catalogue where customers can browse by category, search
      by name or SKU, filter by brand and price, and view full product details
      including specifications and reviews.

   2. Implement a cart and checkout that calculates delivery charges by area,
      applies coupon codes, and accepts Cash on Delivery, bKash, and Nagad.

   3. Create an order management system where customers can track orders and
      admins can move orders through the full lifecycle from pending to delivered.

   4. Build a payment verification workflow so staff can confirm or reject manual
      bKash and Nagad payments using the transaction ID and screenshot the
      customer provides.



                                          1
Orient Computer Ecommerce Website                               Sultana Tasnim Riza


  5. Design an admin dashboard that gives staff an overview of sales, pending
     payments, low-stock products, and recent orders.

  6. Apply security practices appropriate for a production web application: bcrypt
     password hashing, JWT authentication, role-based route protection, input
     sanitization, and server-side validation of all prices, stock levels, and coupon
     rules.


1.3     Scope
The project covers the following areas:

   • Customer registration, login, profile management, and saved addresses

   • Product catalogue with search, filters, sorting, and pagination

   • Product details with multiple images, specifications, and customer reviews

   • Cart management for both guest and registered users

   • Checkout with delivery area selection and COD / bKash / Nagad payment

   • Order history, tracking, and cancellation for customers

   • Wishlist and verified-purchase reviews

   • Admin panel covering products, categories, brands, inventory, orders, cus-
     tomers, coupons, banners, delivery areas, and review moderation

   • Informational pages: About, Contact, Branches, FAQ, and policy pages

The scope does not include an automated payment gateway (this would require
a merchant account with a provider such as SSLCommerz), email or SMS order
notifications, a PC Builder tool, or product comparison. These are noted as possible
future additions.

2.2     Related works
Ecommerce in Bangladesh has grown substantially since mobile internet became
affordable. Most large Bangladeshi retailers (Chaldal, Shajgoj, StarTech, Ryans)
run custom platforms rather than off-the-shelf solutions. The main constraint

specific to the Bangladeshi market is payment: card penetration is low, so bKash
and Nagad are the standard mobile payment options alongside cash on delivery [1].

Star Tech (www.startech.com.bd) and Ryans Computers (www.ryanscomputers.
com) are the largest PC component retailers in Bangladesh with active ecommerce
operations. Both use category trees, product specifications tables, and filtering
patterns similar to international ecommerce sites like Newegg. Orient Computer’s
catalogue structure follows the same pattern, adapted to include product categories
specific to Orient Computer’s business (UPS, IPS, solar inverters, and batteries).

The MERN stack (MongoDB, Express.js, React, Node.js) is a common choice for
projects like this [2]. It keeps both frontend and backend in JavaScript, which
reduces context-switching and means a single developer can work across the full
application. The alternative considered was Django with a React frontend, but
the asynchronous nature of Node.js fits high-concurrency web requests well, and
Mongoose’s schema validation provides enough data safety for this project’s scale.

JSON Web Tokens (JWT) are the standard approach for stateless authentication
in REST APIs [3]. For this project tokens are stored in the client’s localStorage
and sent as a Bearer header. An alternative is HttpOnly cookies, which are harder
to steal via XSS; this is noted as a future improvement.

Tailwind CSS is a utility-first CSS framework that produces consistent, responsive
layouts without writing custom CSS files [4]. It was chosen partly because the
entire site needs to work on mobile, which is the device most Bangladeshi customers
use for online shopping.

4.3        Database design approach
MongoDB uses documents rather than rows. For this application, most entities
are stored as their own collection with references (ObjectId) to related documents.
The main exception is the order document: when an order is created, the product
name, SKU, and price at purchase time are copied into the order document. This
means that if the product is later edited or deactivated, the historical order stays
accurate.

The decision to embed order items inside the order document rather than referencing

                                         9
Orient Computer Ecommerce Website                               Sultana Tasnim Riza


them separately keeps order retrieval to a single query. Since orders are never
mutated except through a controlled status workflow, embedding is safe here.

For the product specifications, each product has an array of key-value pairs
grouped by category (for example, a UPS might have groups “Capacity” and
“Protection”, each with several rows). This structure covers all the product types
Orient Computer sells without requiring a separate specifications table per category.


4.4     API design
The API follows REST conventions. Resource collections use plural nouns. HTTP
methods indicate the action:

                        Table 4.2: Selected API endpoints

 Method     Endpoint                           Description

 POST       /api/auth/register                 Create a customer account
 POST       /api/auth/login                    Login and receive a JWT
 GET        /api/products                      List products (search, filter, sort, paginate)
 GET        /api/products/:id                  Get one product by ID or slug
 POST       /api/cart                          Add an item to the server-side cart
 POST       /api/orders                        Create an order (guest or registered)
 POST       /api/orders/track                  Track an order by number and phone
 GET        /api/admin/dashboard               Admin summary statistics
 PATCH      /api/admin/orders/:id/status       Move order to next status
 PATCH      /api/admin/orders/:id/payment      Verify or reject a payment


Every protected endpoint checks the JWT via a middleware function before the
controller runs. Admin-only endpoints add a second middleware check that verifies
the user’s role is admin. A request from a customer account to an admin endpoint
returns a 403 response.


4.5     Authentication and authorization
When a user logs in, the server signs a JWT containing the user’s database ID.
The token is returned to the client, which stores it in localStorage and attaches it
as a Authorization: Bearer <token> header on all subsequent requests.

The server middleware decodes the token on each request, loads the user from the
database, and attaches the user object to the request. This means role changes

                                         10
Orient Computer Ecommerce Website                             Sultana Tasnim Riza


take effect on the next request without requiring the user to log out and back in.

Passwords are hashed using bcrypt with a cost factor of 12 before storage. The
password field on the User model is set to select: false so it is never included
in query results accidentally.

5.2.2    Non-functional requirements
  1. The application must work on mobile phones, tablets, and desktop browsers.

5.3     System analysis

5.3.1    Feasibility analysis
The application runs on technologies that are well-documented and widely deployed.
Node.js and React have large communities, so finding answers to specific problems
was practical throughout development. MongoDB Atlas handles database hosting
and backups, removing the need to manage a database server.

The main technical risk was the payment flow. Bangladesh does not have a fully
automated mobile money API that any merchant can integrate. The solution was
a manual verification workflow: collect the transaction details from the customer,
let the admin verify them against the company’s bKash or Nagad account, and
update the payment status accordingly. This is how most small Bangladeshi online
stores operate.


5.3.2    Operational feasibility
Orient Computer staff already handle phone-based orders, so the idea of receiving
an online order and confirming it is not new. The admin panel was designed to

                                        14
Orient Computer Ecommerce Website                             Sultana Tasnim Riza


match their existing mental model: orders come in, get confirmed, get packed, get
shipped, get delivered. The status buttons on the order details page make this
workflow explicit.

The main change for staff is the payment verification step for bKash and Nagad
orders. The admin panel shows the transaction ID and screenshot, and provides
Verify and Reject buttons. This is simpler than the current process of checking
WhatsApp screenshots and updating a spreadsheet.


5.3.3    Financial feasibility
The application runs on free and low-cost infrastructure at development scale. As
order volume grows, the main costs are MongoDB Atlas (paid tier at roughly 3,000
BDT/month for a dedicated cluster) and the backend hosting (Railway or Render,
roughly 1,200-2,000 BDT/month for an always-on instance). These costs are well
below the staff hours they replace for any store processing more than a few orders
per day.


5.3.4    Schedule feasibility
The project was completed in approximately three months. The schedule was
feasible because the scope was fixed at the start and new features were added
only after the core workflow (browse, add to cart, checkout, order) was working
end-to-end.

     5.5     Implementation

     5.5.1     Product catalogue and search
     The product listing endpoint (GET /api/products) accepts query parameters for
     category, subcategory, brand, price range, stock status, featured status, sort order,
     and page number. The server builds a MongoDB filter object from these parameters
     and passes it to Product.find().

     Full-text search uses a MongoDB text index on the product name, model, SKU,
     and description fields. For partial matches and brand/category name matching,
     the endpoint also accepts category and brand slugs and resolves them to IDs before
     querying.


     5.5.2     Cart
     Guest cart data is stored as a JSON array in localStorage. When a logged-in user
     adds an item, it goes directly to the server-side Cart document. When a guest logs
     in, the client migrates the localStorage cart to the server by posting each item to
     POST /api/cart, then clears localStorage.


     5.5.3     Order creation
     Order creation is the most critical part of the backend. The server:

        1. Fetches fresh product records for every item in the order.

        2. Checks that no product has callForPrice:           true.

        3. Verifies that stock is sufficient for each item.


                                               17
Orient Computer Ecommerce Website                              Sultana Tasnim Riza


   4. Recalculates the subtotal from server-side prices.

   5. Validates the coupon code (date range, usage limit, minimum purchase) if
      one was submitted.

   6. Looks up the delivery charge from the DeliveryArea collection.

   7. Calculates the final total.

   8. Attempts to wrap the stock decrement in a MongoDB transaction. On a
      standalone Mongo instance (which does not support transactions), it falls
      back to a sequential operation with a manual rollback on failure.

   9. Creates the order document.

 10. Clears the user’s cart.

This sequence ensures the client-supplied total is never used. Every number in the
stored order comes from the database.


5.5.4     Payment verification workflow
When a customer chooses bKash or Nagad at checkout, they enter their sender
phone number and transaction ID, and optionally upload a screenshot. The
payment status is set to submitted.

The admin opens the order, reviews the transaction details, and clicks Verify or
Reject. Verify sets payment.status to verified and records the timestamp and
admin user. Reject stores the reason and sets the status to rejected.

For Cash on Delivery orders, the payment status updates to verified automatically
when the admin marks the order as Delivered.


5.5.5     Order status workflow
Every order has three separate status fields: status (the main lifecycle), deliveryStatus
(shipping progress), and payment.status. The allowed transitions for the main
order status are enforced server-side:

5.5.6    Admin panel
The admin panel is a separate section of the React application, protected by a
route guard that checks both authentication and the admin role. It shares the
same JWT auth as the customer-facing app but uses a different layout.

The dashboard fetches a summary from GET /api/admin/dashboard and displays
total products, total customers, new and completed orders, pending payments,
low-stock count, total sales, and a table of recent orders.


5.6     Testing
Testing covered three main workflows manually, verified against the live Atlas
database.

Customer workflow: Browse products, add to cart, complete checkout with each
payment method (COD, bKash, Nagad), view the confirmation page, view the
order in account history, and track it using the order number and phone.

Admin workflow: Log in, view the dashboard, open a new order, verify the
payment, move the order through each status from Confirmed to Delivered, and
confirm the customer’s delivery status updates.

Security workflow: Attempt to access /admin as a logged-out user (redirected
to login), as a customer account (redirected to home), and with a fabricated JWT
(API returns 401). Attempt to submit a checkout with a manipulated total from

Chapter 7        Project as Engineering Problem Analysis


7.1     Sustainability of the project
The application is built with standard, widely-maintained open-source libraries.
Node.js, React, and MongoDB all have long-term commercial backing and active
communities. This means the application is unlikely to become unmaintainable
due to an abandoned dependency within a normal product lifespan.

The codebase is split into small, focused files (each controller, model, and component
handles one concern), which makes it straightforward for another developer to take
over or extend the project. Environment variables separate configuration from
code, so the application can be moved to a different host without code changes.

MongoDB Atlas handles automated backups on its paid tier. For the free tier used
during development, periodic manual exports of the database were taken using
mongodump.


7.2     Social and environmental effects
Giving Orient Computer an online sales channel reduces the need for customers
in other districts to travel to Dhaka to buy products. A customer in Sylhet or
Chittagong can order online and receive delivery within three to five days, rather
than making a trip or relying on a third party to purchase on their behalf.

For Orient Computer, an online store means the sales team can handle more orders
per day without proportionally increasing staff, because the customer fills in their
own shipping details and payment information. Staff time shifts from taking phone
orders to verifying payments and updating order statuses.

The application generates no physical waste. Server infrastructure (MongoDB
Atlas, Vercel, Railway) runs on shared data centre hardware with higher utilization
rates than dedicated servers, which is generally more energy-efficient per request.

One concern specific to the Bangladeshi context is digital literacy. Customers
who are not comfortable ordering online will not use this application. The store
complements rather than replaces the showroom and phone channels for those
customers.

Chapter 8        Lessons Learned


8.1     Problems faced
MongoDB transactions on a standalone instance. The order creation logic
uses a MongoDB transaction to make the stock decrement and order insert atomic.
This works on MongoDB Atlas (which runs as a replica set) but fails silently on
a standalone local MongoDB installation, because transactions require a replica
set. During early development I was testing on local Mongo, which meant the
transaction code was never actually executing. The fix was to add a fallback
path that does the operations sequentially on a standalone instance and also tests
against Atlas from the start.

Guest cart and login migration. When a guest adds items to the cart and
then logs in, the cart should merge into their account. The initial implementation
wiped the server cart and replaced it with the guest cart. This caused problems
when a user had items from a previous session on the server. The correct approach
is to add each guest item to the server cart individually, which lets the server check
stock and merge quantities correctly.

Coupon edge cases. Several coupon edge cases were not obvious from the initial
requirements. What happens if the minimum purchase is met at cart time but the
coupon expires before the order is placed? What if the usage limit is hit between
cart and checkout? The fix was to re-validate the coupon fully at order creation
time on the server, not just at cart time.

File upload path handling on Windows. Multer stores files using the OS path
separator. On Windows this produces backslashes in file paths, which then get
sent to the client and fail to match the forward-slash URL paths that Express uses
to serve the uploads directory. The fix was to normalize all stored paths to use
forward slashes before saving them to the database.

Admin panel category tree. The admin product form needs to load the full
category tree so the user can select a parent category and then a subcategory. The
initial implementation loaded categories with the GET /api/categories endpoint,
which only returns active parent categories with their subcategories. The admin
form needed inactive categories too, and needed them flat rather than nested. The
fix was to add a separate GET /api/categories/all endpoint that returns all


                                         27
Orient Computer Ecommerce Website                            Sultana Tasnim Riza


categories in a flat list, protected to admin only.


8.2     Solutions and what I would do differently
The main thing I would do differently is test against the actual Atlas deployment
earlier. Several bugs (the transaction issue, a few timing issues with async cart
operations) only surfaced when running against a real network connection rather
than a local server.

I would also write the coupon and delivery charge validation as separate service
functions from the start, rather than putting them inside the order controller.
Keeping business rules in service files made them easier to test and reuse, but I
only refactored them there after encountering the edge cases described above.

For the frontend, using React Query or SWR instead of manual useEffect data
fetching would reduce the amount of loading state management code. This is
something I plan to explore in the next project.

Chapter 9        Future Work and Conclusion


9.1     Future work
Several features were out of scope for this internship but are realistic near-term
additions.

Automated payment gateway. SSLCommerz and ShurjoPay both offer Bangladeshi
merchant accounts that support bKash, Nagad, cards, and other local methods
through a single API. Replacing the manual verification workflow with an auto-
mated gateway would reduce admin work and improve the customer experience.
This requires a merchant account and additional security work (webhook signature
verification, idempotency keys) that were out of scope for this project.

Email and SMS notifications. Customers currently have to check the website
or the admin has to call them to communicate order status changes. Sending
an SMS via a service like Twilio or a local provider when an order is confirmed,
shipped, or delivered would be a significant improvement.

PC Builder. Many Bangladeshi computer shoppers want to select compatible
components and check if a build stays within budget. A PC Builder tool is a stan-
dard feature on Star Tech and Ryans. It requires compatibility rules (motherboard
socket vs CPU socket, RAM type, etc.) and real-time price totalling.

Product comparison. Customers buying UPS systems or solar inverters often
want to compare specifications side by side. Adding a comparison view for up to
three or four products would help.

HttpOnly cookie authentication. Storing the JWT in an HttpOnly cookie
instead of localStorage protects it from XSS attacks. This requires CSRF protection
and a small change to how the API serves the token, but it is a meaningful security
improvement for a production deployment.


9.2     Conclusion
The Orient Computer ecommerce website works as intended. A customer can
browse 17 seeded products across 8 categories (31 including subcategories), add
items to a cart, check out with Cash on Delivery or manual bKash/Nagad, and


                                        29
Orient Computer Ecommerce Website                                Sultana Tasnim Riza


track their order. An admin can confirm orders, verify payments, manage the full
product catalogue, and configure delivery charges by district.

All the business rules specified in the SRS run on the server: stock is checked before
the order is accepted, prices are frozen at purchase time, coupons are validated
against the database, and customers cannot cancel orders that are already in
processing or shipping. Customers cannot access any admin function regardless of
how they modify the frontend.

The application is ready to be deployed to production once a domain name is
registered, the frontend is pointed at the production API URL, and the Atlas
cluster is upgraded to a paid tier for backup and performance guarantees.

The three months spent on this project covered a wider range of web development
topics than any single university course. Writing the payment verification workflow
and the order status machine gave me a clearer understanding of how business
logic and HTTP handling should be separated, and debugging the Mongoose
transaction behaviour taught me more about how MongoDB actually works than
the documentation alone would have.
